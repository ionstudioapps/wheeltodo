Gate AI for conversion value, not cost recovery. Our AI is astonishingly cheap to run — less than 1% of revenue even at scale — so the free tier can be generous. Monetize the bundle (wheel + gamification + rest mode + unlimited AI) with a freemium subscription at ~$4.99/mo or ~$29.99/yr, lead with monthly, keep a small free AI allowance, and treat ads as (at most) an optional "watch-to-refill" nudge. ⚠ The real gap in "voice input"

Our voice-tasks function takes a text transcript as input — it does not convert speech to text. The audio → transcript step (the hard part of "voice input") does not exist yet. Section 3 is about closing that gap, and the good news is it can be done for $0.

Other relevant facts about the current build: all task/streak/history data lives in localStorage/AsyncStorage; Supabase is used only for auth. There are no subscription or entitlement tables yet — monetization infrastructure is greenfield, which means we get to design it correctly from the start (likely via RevenueCat, already floated in IDEAS.md).

Recommendation

Move break-task and voice-tasks off Sonnet. The pragmatic first step is Haiku 4.5 — a one-line model-string change, 3× cheaper, no new vendor, and our suggest-category function already proves Haiku is reliable for this. If we want to drive AI COGS to near-zero (and possibly free via the free tier), Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite is ~37× cheaper than Sonnet; use its JSON-schema mode to guarantee valid output. Keep Sonnet/Opus only if we later add a genuinely hard feature (e.g. multi-step planning). Quality risk is low — these are short, well-constrained JSON tasks.

3Voice input — closing the speech-to-text gap

"Voice input" needs two steps: audio → transcript (STT, which we haven't built) then transcript → tasks (which we have). For short voice notes, the first step can be free.

The free path: on-device speech recognition

Both mobile OSes ship a speech recognizer we can call for $0, no API key, often offline:

  • Mobile (Expo/React Native): use expo-speech-recognition (or @react-native-voice/voice) to reach iOS SFSpeechRecognizer and Android SpeechRecognizer. On-device transcription works on iOS 17+ and Android 13+ (Android needs a one-time offline-model download). Cost: zero.
  • Web (Next.js): the browser Web Speech API (SpeechRecognition) is free in Chrome/Edge/Safari. Caveat: it streams audio to Google/Apple servers (not truly offline) and sessions auto-end ~60s — fine for short notes, but handle the restart/no-speech cases.

Caveats: native STT quality varies by device, OS version, and noise; very short single-word utterances recognize poorly; Android emulators are unreliable (test on real devices). So we want a cloud fallback when the native path is unavailable or returns low confidence.

Recommendation

Hybrid: native STT first, cheap cloud fallback. Default to on-device recognition (free) on both mobile and web — for short notes on modern devices it's good enough and costs nothing, so voice can be a generous free-tier hook. Fall back to OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-transcribe (~0.15¢/note, and it consolidates billing if we also run the LLM step on OpenAI) when native is unavailable or low-confidence. Reach for ElevenLabs Scribe only if accuracy becomes the product's limiting factor.

4What AI actually costs us

This is the most important section for the pricing debate, because it overturns the usual assumption that "AI features are expensive, so we must charge to cover them."

Modelling a moderately active user at 8 subtask breakdowns + 12 voice notes + 40 auto-category calls per month [estimate — adjust once we have real usage telemetry]:

Current stack Sonnet + cloud STT$1.33/yr
Optimized Flash-Lite + native STT$0.16/yr
ScenarioPer user / moPer user / yrAt 10,000 active users% of $30/yr sub
Current (Sonnet text, cloud STT)11.1¢$1.33~$13,300 / yr4.4%
Optimized (Flash-Lite text, native STT)1.3¢$0.16~$1,600 / yr0.5%
The key insight

Even before optimizing, AI is ~4% of a subscription's revenue; optimized, it's well under 1%. The only real cost risk is abuse (someone scripting thousands of calls) — not normal usage. That means: (1) the free tier can afford to be generous, which is great for acquisition; (2) a free-tier cap exists to stop abuse and create an upgrade nudge, not to recover cost; and (3) native voice STT removes the one usage-scaling cloud cost entirely.

Positioning read

Our wheel mechanic is uncontested — no researched competitor uses a randomized spinner to pick the next task, and it maps perfectly to ADHD task-initiation paralysis. That's the wedge. But the AI features are not differentiators: subtask breakdown is free at Goblin Tools, and voice capture is sliding into free tiers (Todoist Ramble). So: lead the story with the wheel + gamification + guilt-free rest mode, use AI as a delight/retention layer, and price in the consumer ADHD/gamified band of ~$25–70/yr — not the $250–350/yr pro band.

Funnel & pricing patterns that work in 2026

  • Onboarding paywall (shown during first-run, with a trial) is the highest-converting placement — ~1.35% download→paid on average.
  • Three options on the paywall (monthly / annual / free-continue) is the optimal default.
  • Lead with monthly — productivity users resist annual lock-in (77% choose monthly) — but offer annual at ~40–50% off the monthly run-rate to capture committers.
  • Free trials: longer trials (2–4 weeks) convert far better (~42–46%) than 3-day trials (~25%), but 3-day trials see ~55% cancel on day 0. If we trial, trial the annual plan.
Honest caveats on these numbers

The exact productivity-vertical free→paid % and LTV medians sit behind RevenueCat's paid report, so the 2–4% range is the cross-category baseline. There is no clean published utility-app ARPDAU benchmark (the "below $0.05" is inferred). And the annual-discount norm (40–50%) is a design convention, not a single cited statistic. Treat these as direction, not gospel.

7Recommendation & next steps

A concrete proposal we can argue about. Numbers are starting points, not commitments.

Proposed model: generous freemium + thin AI cap

WheelTodo Free
$0
  • Full core app: wheel, tasks, streaks, achievements, Pomodoro, rest mode
  • Voice capture: unlimited (native STT is free to us — make it a hook)
  • AI subtask breakdown: ~10 / month, then a soft prompt to upgrade (or watch-to-refill)
  • Default themes; standard rest categories
WheelTodo Plus
$4.99 /mo
  • Unlimited AI subtask breakdown & voice→tasks
  • Custom rest categories, mood & focus trends, wheel bias
  • Extra themes, widgets, weekly "coach" summary
  • Annual: ~$29.99/yr (50% off) · optional one-time "Supporter" unlock

Why this shape: the wheel + gamification are the paid story; AI is a delight feature with a thin cap that exists for abuse-control and upgrade nudging (not cost — AI is <1% of revenue). Voice stays unlimited and free because native STT costs us nothing and it's a superb low-friction ADHD acquisition hook. Pricing sits in the proven $25–70/yr consumer-ADHD band, monthly-led per the benchmarks.

Build sequence

  1. Cut AI COGS now (1 line): switch break-task and voice-tasks from Sonnet 4.6 → Haiku 4.5. Optionally pilot Gemini Flash-Lite for near-zero cost.
  2. Build the STT layer: on-device speech recognition (mobile + web), with an OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-transcribe fallback. This is what makes "voice input" actually exist.
  3. Add entitlement infra: RevenueCat + a Supabase entitlements/usage table (none exists today). Meter AI actions per user/month.
  4. Ship the paywall: onboarding placement, 3 options, monthly-led, annual at ~50% off, short trial on annual.
  5. Instrument, then tune: our cost model uses assumed usage — replace it with real telemetry and re-set the free cap and price from data.
Open questions for the team

(1) Is the free AI cap 10/month or should voice-everything be free and only subtask-AI capped? (2) Do we want a one-time "Supporter" unlock alongside the subscription (ADHD-indie goodwill) or keep it subscription-only? (3) Launch price: hold $4.99/mo or test $5.99? (4) Are we comfortable adding Google/OpenAI as a second AI vendor, or stay all-Anthropic on Haiku for simplicity?
